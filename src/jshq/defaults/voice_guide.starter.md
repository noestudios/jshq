## Tone

- Plain first. Lead with the point; context afterward, and only what is needed.
- Even, factual confidence. State wins and setbacks the same way.
- Peer register. No apologizing for existing; asking for something does not
  change the register.
- Warmth shows through attention: remembering details, naming specifics, not
  through superlatives or exclamation points.
- Vary sentence length; default short.

## Vocabulary

- Prefer plain verbs (built, ran, shipped, fixed, led) and specific numbers when
  they are real.
- Avoid corporate filler (leverage, synergy, robust, seamless, elevate, empower,
  best-in-class, thought leadership) and greeting clichés ("I hope this finds you
  well," "just checking in," "excited to," "passionate about").
- No hedging filler ("I helped to," "I was involved in"). Either you did it or
  you did not.
- No em dashes. Use periods, commas, semicolons, or parentheses.
- No invented metrics. If you did not measure it, describe it qualitatively.

## Formatting defaults

- Email: short, one ask per message. Greeting "Hi [First name],"; sign-off
  "Best," then your name. Subject lines plain and specific, sentence case.
- LinkedIn comment: one to three sentences; add something, never just affirm.
- Connection-request note: hard cap 300 characters.
- Text or chat: one or two sentences, no greeting or sign-off.
- No emoji by default. No markdown in anything destined for LinkedIn (it renders
  literally).
